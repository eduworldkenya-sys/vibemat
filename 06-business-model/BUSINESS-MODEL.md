# Business Model and Money Boundaries

## Agreed money boundary

VibeMat will not handle passenger fares or transport money.

It will not:
- Collect passenger fares.
- Hold transport funds.
- Operate fare escrow.
- Settle fares between passengers and providers.
- Pay out drivers, touts, agents, or operators.
- Maintain a transport wallet for fare custody.
- Take a percentage of a passenger fare through payment processing.

Passengers pay the transport provider directly using existing channels such as cash, M-Pesa Till, Pochi, or another direct arrangement.

## What VibeMat may do

VibeMat may help users discover activity, inspect details, connect with providers, and record non-financial product events such as views, saves, enquiries, requests, and outcomes—subject to privacy, consent, and product design.

VibeMat may record attribution or contribution without taking custody of the underlying transport money.

## Important qualification

Avoiding fare custody does not automatically remove all legal, consumer-protection, data-protection, transport, advertising, or other regulatory obligations. Those must be assessed as the product and operating model become clearer.

## Open business questions

- What value will be free to passengers?
- What optional paid services, if any, might be offered to operators or businesses?
- Could verified business tools or promoted discovery exist without compromising trust?
- How should paid visibility be labelled and governed?
- What is the business model that does not depend on taking a cut of passenger fares?

No pricing or monetisation model is approved yet.

## Morning decision-support and commercial neutrality

The passenger's morning briefing is a core product-value experience, not automatically an advertising slot. Any future paid promotion must be clearly labelled and must not secretly influence factual traffic, stage-status, or availability information.

VibeMat must not sell a false claim of availability or allow a paid operator to appear verified merely because they paid. Commercial ranking rules remain open and must preserve trust.


## The VibeMat network: who participates and what each person gets

VibeMat is one connected network with different participant roles. These are product roles, not necessarily separate apps or rigid account types. One person may hold more than one role, with permissions and identity checks appropriate to each activity.

### 1. Passengers and transport seekers

**Need:** understand what is happening, compare options, discover journeys and opportunities, and make better-informed decisions.

**Value received:** nearby stage and route intelligence, planned journeys, credible reports, alternatives, saved places/routes, followed sources, and useful updates.

**Contribution:** optional observations, confirmations, corrections, demand signals, and sharing useful information.

**Default commercial treatment:** core passenger discovery remains free. Do not charge people simply to see essential safety, disruption, or basic transport information.

### 2. Drivers, conductors, touts and stage contributors

**Need:** make transport activity visible, communicate accurate updates, attract relevant demand, and build a trusted contribution history.

**Value received:** discovery of their declared activity, audience for useful updates, contribution analytics, and possible commercial opportunities from operators.

**Contribution:** departure and capacity declarations, stage observations, route changes, disruptions, and updates. They may publish only within their authority and permissions.

**Earning opportunity:** a driver/conductor/tout or stage contributor may be paid directly by an operator or SACCO for an agreed promotion, verified update, or customer-acquisition service. VibeMat may attribute the result but does not hold or pay out transport money.

### 3. Transport creators / route MCs

These are people who make transport information easy to understand and discover—such as route explainers, stage reporters, live departure presenters, local mobility guides, and transport storytellers.

**Need:** an audience, credibility, discoverability, and legitimate ways to earn.

**Value received:** creator profile, follows, reach analytics, publishing tools, attribution for enquiries or fulfilled outcomes where measurable, and access to operator-sponsored assignments.

**Content examples:** “What is moving from Eastmar this morning?”, a verified route disruption explainer, a live operator-authorised departure update, or a comparison of available route options.

**Guardrail:** no fabricated urgency, staged scarcity, unsafe filming, harassment, or unverified claims presented as fact. A creator's popularity does not make their reports true.

### 4. SACCOs, fleet owners and transport operators

**Need:** reach passengers, publish reliable activity, improve utilisation, communicate changes, and understand demand patterns.

**Value received:** operator profile and verification, activity publishing tools, fleet/route management views, qualified enquiry attribution, performance analytics, optional promoted discovery, and aggregated intelligence.

**Potential payer:** operator subscription or paid business tools; clearly labelled promotion; optional campaign or qualified-lead services if the outcome can be measured.

**Guardrail:** payment cannot buy a verified badge, false availability, or a favourable factual status. Paid placements must be visibly labelled and separated from organic trust signals.

### 5. Parcel senders, traders and cargo customers

**Need:** discover transport activity that may carry parcels or goods, identify route overlap and capacity declarations, and contact a suitable provider.

**Value received:** discovery of relevant journeys, declared capacity, route overlap, and provider contact where enabled.

**Potential payer:** business tools or qualified discovery services for frequent commercial users, subject to testing. Do not charge merely for seeing a public transport declaration.

### 6. Merchants and local businesses

Examples include shops, markets, garages, fuel/service businesses, food outlets, and businesses located near transport hubs.

**Need:** reach people travelling through relevant areas and understand aggregate transport-related footfall or route patterns.

**Value received:** clearly labelled local promotions, profile/discovery placement, and privacy-safe aggregate insights where data quality and group thresholds permit.

**Potential payer:** advertising or business subscription. Never sell identifiable passenger location histories or expose individual routines.

### 7. Public bodies, researchers and mobility partners

**Need:** understand network-level patterns, disruptions, demand and mobility changes.

**Value received:** privacy-protected aggregate reports, dashboards or research access under strict governance.

**Potential payer:** data/insight services or institutional contracts, only where collection, aggregation, lawful basis, accuracy and permitted use are established.

Do not sell raw personal data, individual travel histories, or precise passenger traces.

## Dashboard interpretation: one platform, role-aware views

The dashboard should not show the same metrics to every account. The map remains the shared network canvas, while the supporting dashboard changes according to the user's role and permissions.

### Passenger dashboard

- What is happening around my selected area?
- What planned journeys or useful opportunities are available?
- What changed on routes and stages I follow?
- Which reports are fresh, estimated, declared, or verified?
- What have I saved, and what should I inspect next?

### Operator / SACCO dashboard

- Which activities and departures have been published?
- Which declarations need updating or have expired?
- How many people were reached in aggregate?
- Which enquiries or contacts were attributed to this activity?
- Which campaigns or paid placements are active?
- What route/stage patterns are visible in privacy-safe aggregate form?

Do not equate views with passengers, enquiries with bookings, or bookings with completed journeys.

### Driver / conductor / stage contributor dashboard

- What activity have I published?
- Which items need an update or correction?
- How many aggregate views/reaches did my updates receive?
- Which reports were independently confirmed or disputed?
- Which operator has assigned or sponsored a task?
- What contribution history and reliability evidence have I earned?

No public individual passenger tracking or viewer identity list.

### Transport creator / MC dashboard

- Audience/follow growth.
- Aggregate reach and useful engagement.
- Published route/stage updates and their status.
- Confirmations, disputes and corrections.
- Attributed enquiries or completed outcomes, where verifiable.
- Eligible operator campaigns and agreed compensation terms.

Do not reward creators solely for watch time, sensationalism or raw views.

### Merchant dashboard

- Promotion reach in the selected area/category.
- Aggregate visits or enquiries only where consented and measurable.
- Campaign status and spend.
- Privacy-safe area-level trends.

### Public / research partner dashboard

- Aggregate corridor and stage patterns.
- Time-bounded trends and disruption summaries.
- Data coverage, confidence, freshness and methodology.
- No individual passenger identities or precise personal movement histories.

## Attention, intent, outcome and trust: the VibeMat measurement model

Keep separate measurement dimensions:
- **Attention:** impressions/reach, views, and follows. These indicate exposure or a continuing interest relationship. A follow is a stronger preference signal than a view, but neither proves truth or commercial demand.
- **Intent:** saves/pins, route follows, enquiries, contact requests, and other deliberate next steps. A save is not a booking.
- **Outcome:** a verified or credibly recorded event such as boarding, completed delivery, fulfilled operator request, or resolved service interaction. Outcomes require a defined evidence method.
- **Trust evidence:** independent confirmations, disputes, corrections, source history and verification. This is a separate dimension—not a popularity score.

A useful funnel is:

**Discovery → Attention → Intent → Contact/coordination → Outcome → Repeat use**

Measure conversion between stages. Never report a view as a passenger, a save as demand, an enquiry as a booking, or a declared journey as a completed trip.

## How VibeMat makes money without taking passenger fares

The following are recommended business-model candidates, not approved prices or proven revenue streams.

### A. Operator subscriptions and business tools

Charge transport organisations for valuable operating tools beyond basic public discovery: fleet/activity management, publishing workflows, multi-user access, campaign reporting, and performance analytics.

Start only after demonstrating that these tools solve a recurring operator problem.

### B. Clearly labelled sponsored discovery

Operators, route services and relevant local businesses may pay for sponsored placement in defined contexts.

Rules:
- Label every paid placement.
- Keep paid distribution separate from factual verification and organic trust.
- Never sell a false “available now”, “verified”, “moving”, or “safe” claim.
- Users must be able to distinguish sponsored from organic results.
- Safety, disruption and essential public information must not be hidden behind payment.

### C. Qualified lead and campaign services

An operator or business may pay for a measurable, qualified enquiry or a campaign outcome. Attribution can be recorded without collecting the fare or holding the customer's money.

Define what counts as a qualified enquiry, prevent duplicate/fraudulent attribution, and distinguish enquiry, booking, boarding and completion. Any fee arrangement must be transparent to the business.

### D. Creator and local transport promotion network

Operators may hire route MCs, stage contributors or transport creators for specific campaigns or verified information tasks. The operator pays the creator directly under their agreement. VibeMat may charge the operator for campaign tools/attribution or a disclosed service fee, but does not operate a creator wallet or payout system in the initial model.

Do not promise creator earnings before there is paying demand.

### E. Privacy-safe transport intelligence

Offer aggregated mobility insights to operators, merchants, institutions, researchers or planning partners when there is sufficient coverage, lawful basis, privacy protection and documented methodology.

Do not sell raw device traces, identifiable passenger data, or unsupported predictions.

### F. Local business advertising

Relevant businesses may pay to reach transport audiences by area or context. Advertising must be labelled, frequency-controlled and separated from safety-critical and factual transport information.

## Recommended free-versus-paid boundary

**Keep free for passengers:**
- Basic map and public transport discovery.
- Essential disruption and safety information.
- Basic stage, route and journey details.
- Source, time, freshness and evidence status.
- Following and private saving of useful information.
- Reporting incorrect or unsafe information.

**Potentially paid for businesses/operators:**
- Advanced publishing and management tools.
- Multi-user and fleet workflows.
- Campaigns and clearly labelled promotion.
- Qualified lead attribution and analytics.
- Privacy-safe aggregate intelligence.
- Business reporting and integrations.

The network needs passenger usefulness and broad participation before aggressive monetisation. Charging passengers for basic discovery too early would weaken the network effect.

## How VibeMat keeps people returning

The goal is useful repeat behaviour, not addiction or artificial screen time.

People have reasons to return because the transport network changes:
- A followed operator publishes a new departure.
- A stage contributor reports a disruption.
- A route changes or a new alternative appears.
- A journey with relevant capacity is declared.
- A saved route receives a meaningful update.
- A creator explains a route or transport issue.
- A user has a new trip, parcel, delivery or business need.
- The user's own contribution receives a correction or confirmation.

### Four types of value in the product

1. **Information:** know what is happening, where, when, from whom, and how fresh it is.
2. **Opportunity:** discover a ride, cargo connection, customer, job, promotion, service or business lead.
3. **Earning:** let eligible contributors and creators earn from real operator-funded work or measurable commercial value, paid directly by the contracting party.
4. **Enjoyment / entertainment:** make useful transport information engaging through clear route explainers, live authorised updates, human transport stories, local discoveries and creator-led reporting.

Entertainment is a presentation layer around real transport value. Do not manufacture incidents, encourage dangerous filming, or reward misinformation for views.

### Healthy engagement rules

- Notifications are opt-in, useful, timely and controllable.
- No infinite-scroll mechanic should be required for the product to work.
- Do not use streaks, manipulative urgency, or engagement bait to create unnecessary checking.
- Give users clear ways to finish a task and leave.
- Do not make attention metrics the sole basis for creator compensation or distribution.
- Protect quiet periods and let users control followed topics and notification frequency.

## Network effects and defensibility

VibeMat becomes more useful as more legitimate participants contribute current, trustworthy transport information:

1. Operators publish journeys and service updates.
2. Contributors report stage and road conditions.
3. Passengers discover, follow, save, confirm and correct information.
4. Creators make useful transport information easier to understand and discover.
5. Businesses and operators receive measurable demand and insights.
6. More participants have a reason to contribute, because the network helps them find information, customers or opportunities.

The defensible asset is not a copied feed interface. It is the connected transport activity graph: places, stages, routes, operators, vehicles, declarations, reports, evidence, relationships, outcomes and history—collected with permission and governed by trust.

## Could TikTok buy VibeMat?

It is possible in principle for a large social platform to partner with or acquire a specialised network, but copying TikTok's feed is not a credible acquisition thesis by itself. There is no basis to assume TikTok would buy VibeMat.

A stronger strategic case would be that VibeMat owns or operates something difficult to reproduce quickly:
- A meaningful, permissioned transport information network.
- Strong operator, contributor and passenger participation.
- Reliable transport-specific discovery and evidence systems.
- A creator ecosystem that produces useful, locally grounded transport information.
- Demonstrated demand distribution and measurable outcomes for transport businesses.
- Repeat use and defensible network coverage across important corridors.
- Clear privacy, safety, governance and regulatory practices.

Potential strategic relationships could include integration, distribution partnership, commercial partnership, investment, licensing or acquisition. These are possibilities, not a roadmap dependency.

Build VibeMat to be valuable and sustainable as an independent company. Do not build it merely to be bought. If it becomes strategically valuable to TikTok or another platform, that value should arise from real users, trusted data, network effects and commercial results—not resemblance to another app.

## Business-model validation sequence

1. Prove passengers return because information and opportunities are genuinely useful.
2. Prove operators publish accurate, timely activities because they receive value.
3. Prove contributors and creators can improve coverage and quality.
4. Measure whether discovery generates qualified enquiries or completed outcomes.
5. Test one operator-paid tool or campaign with a real paying customer.
6. Add more revenue streams only when value, cost to serve, trust and compliance are understood.

Do not assume advertising, subscriptions, creator commissions or data products will work until tested with actual customers. No prices, revenue targets or acquisition assumptions are approved by this document.


## Confirmed first monetisation experiment

The first commercial experiment will target **SACCOs and transport operators**. The initial value proposition is **helping an operator reach more passengers**.

The first test should be deliberately narrow:
- One participating operator.
- One defined route or corridor.
- One measurable campaign.
- Basic passenger map and transport discovery remain free.
- VibeMat does not collect fares, hold passenger funds, or settle transport payments.

The experiment must measure genuine reach and downstream value separately. Impressions/views are attention, not passengers. Enquiries are intent, not bookings. Boarding or completed journeys count as outcomes only when a credible measurement method exists.

This is the agreed starting hypothesis, not proof of willingness to pay. Pricing, campaign format, attribution rules, success thresholds, and the exact operator/route remain to be validated before launch.
